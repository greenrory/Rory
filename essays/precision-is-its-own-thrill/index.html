Precision Is Its Own Thrill.

06.20.26

There are two types of drivers. One gets a thrill out of going fast - pushing the limits, cutting red lights close, and swimming through cars. The other gets a kick out of driving perfectly - ensuring every turn is smooth, staying the most aware, and optimizing every input. Both are chasing the same feeling - a feeling of mastery and being alive.

There is a road to obtain that feeling, and it diverges into two distinct paths. Each driver (person) chooses their path. Instead of looking for shortcuts to the end result, one can focus on each input along the way. Instead of choosing the immediate pleasure of a quick rush, one can focus on the journey and all its intricacies.

The precision driver gets to feel that mastery every single mile - not just in one big risky rush, but in every turn and input - the whole way. The fast driver's thrill is over in a second. The precision driver's thrill lasts the entire drive.

And that's true of everything, not just driving. You can chase the quick rush of shortcuts, or you can find the quieter thrill of doing the thing as well as it can be done - over and over, for as long as you do it. Your satisfaction with the intentionality of your actions will stay with you not just for the ride, but in life as a whole - as the way.

Why does the fast driver's thrill fade but the precision driver's compound?

Like the scent of a candle slowly burning with a strong, steady flame - the person who focuses on optimized inputs compounds and becomes stronger in their space. Those sparkler candles, like the ones people put on birthday cakes, contain metals like magnesium that ignite in a quick spark. Once the spark finishes, it stops producing airborne particles immediately, which is why it doesn't leave a lasting scent. The same can be said about the person who seeks a quick rush - it is over then and there, often leaving them with a drop in their satisfaction after.

What if we could feel that sense of satisfaction and rush with our work and the things we do at all times - even if nothing particularly exciting is happening - because doing things we love involves mundane tasks, and ensuring all of those inputs are managed the best we can will lead us to achieving the thing we set out to do. Precision lets you feel progression and mastery in the tedious parts of life, so there is a hidden pleasure in doing things the best they can be done.
